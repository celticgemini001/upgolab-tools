How to make a blurry video sharp

Blurry video has four different causes. Each one needs different treatment, and one of them cannot be fixed at all. Diagnose before you process.

Fix by cause, not by tool

CauseRecoverable?What to do
Compressed by a messaging app (block grid, dark-area noise)Mostly yesDenoise for block artifacts, then enlarge max 2x
Low light, high ISOPartlyDenoise first, then enlarge. Accept flatter skin
Camera shake, whip panBarelyPick a motion-tolerant preset, do not over-sharpen
True out-of-focusNoNothing. The sensor never recorded the detail

Diagnose in ten seconds

  1. Pull one frame at full resolution.
  2. View it at 100%, not fitted to the window.
  3. Look at a hard edge — a hairline, a text stroke, a window frame.

Structure present means it can probably be sharpened. No structure at all means it is focus blur, and no tool will invent the real detail back. Stop there and save the time.

The most common wasted hour: running three different upscalers on footage that was simply out of focus, then comparing them. They will all produce the same softened result because the information is not in the file.

The order that works

  1. Denoise, targeting compression artifacts
  2. Enlarge at most 2x per axis
  3. Inspect at 100% on the hair edge
  4. Grade last

Enlarging first teaches the model the compression blocks and enlarges them too. The result looks sharp until you put it next to the source.

Why 2x is the ceiling

Past roughly 2x the model switches from reconstructing to inventing. Faces go waxy, small text becomes strokes that resemble letters without being them. A 540p source at 2x lands at 1080p, which is enough for anything watched on a screen. The 4K and 8K labels describe output resolution, not recovered information.

Compression noise is not grain. Compression imposes a regular block grid. Sensor grain is random. A generic denoiser treats the grid as noise and leaves it in place, then the upscaler amplifies it. This distinction changed results more than any model choice.

Long clips: split at keyframes

Segmenting without re-encoding keeps the source intact:

ffmpeg -i input.mp4 -c copy -f segment -segment_time 30 -reset_timestamps 1 seg-%03d.mp4

Process each segment, then rejoin:

printf "file '%s'\n" seg-*.mp4 > list.txt
ffmpeg -f concat -safe 0 -i list.txt -c output.mp4

Frequently asked

How do you fix a blurry video?

Identify the cause first. Compression blur responds to denoising then 2x enlargement. Low-light blur needs denoising before anything else. Motion blur barely responds. True focus blur is permanent.

Can AI make an out-of-focus video sharp?

Only partly. AI reconstructs plausible detail, it does not recover the real detail. On a soft subject it produces convincing but invented texture.

What is the maximum useful upscale factor?

About 2x per axis. Past that the model invents rather than reconstructs.

How do you remove compression blocks from a video?

Treat them as structure, not noise. Denoise for block artifacts specifically, then enlarge.

How do you upscale a video longer than the tool allows?

Split at keyframes, process each segment, rejoin with concat. Cutting on keyframes keeps the seam invisible.
